**Adaptive IoT-UAV Environmental Monitoring System with Downwash-Aware Sensing and Adaptive Communication**

## 2. Motivation & Problem Statement (Relevance & Necessity)

### 2.1. Practical Motivation
Traditional ground-based environmental monitoring stations are inherently constrained by fixed spatial placement. They lack the mobility required to monitor complex terrains, variable vertical atmospheric gradients, or localized microclimates. Integrating environmental sensors into Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs / Drones) provides agile 3D spatial sensing capabilities.

However, conventional implementations that simply mount sensors onto a commercial drone and stream raw data encounter two fundamental technical bottlenecks:

### 2.2. Core Problem 1: Measurement Disturbance Caused by Propeller Downwash (Sensing Problem)
* **Aerodynamic Turbulence:** When a UAV operates, vigorous propeller-induced downwash directly strikes the sensor enclosure.
* **Thermal Coupling:** Heat dissipation generated by on-board electronics (ESP32 microcontroller, LiPo battery, ESCs, and flight controller) is drawn into the airflow stream, potentially causing transient local microclimate shifts around the temperature and relative humidity sensor (BME280).
* **Consequence:** Without proper shielding, raw measurements can diverge from ground-truth environmental conditions. Furthermore, improperly designed protective enclosures can induce thermal trapping, worsening measurement errors.

### 2.3. Core Problem 2: Volatile Aerial Wireless Transmission & Resource Constraints (Communication Problem)
* **Dynamic Link Quality:** The wireless radio channel (Wi-Fi / MQTT) between an airborne UAV and the ground station fluctuates continuously due to distance variations, banking angles, and radio shadowing (reflected in volatile Received Signal Strength Indicator - RSSI).
* **Limitations of Conventional Fixed-Rate Telemetry:**
  - Periodic fixed-rate transmission (e.g., 1 Hz constant streaming) transmits redundant data packets during steady environmental states, draining scarce onboard battery energy and increasing network contention.
  - When the UAV enters weak RSSI zones, high-frequency transmission causes channel congestion, buffer overflow, and severe packet loss.
  - Conversely, statically lowering the transmission frequency degrades the Age of Information (AoI) and risks missing critical transient environmental events.

### 2.4. Project Necessity
This project is necessary because it delivers a holistic, research-oriented IoT system addressing both measurement fidelity under aerodynamic interference and communication efficiency over unreliable aerial links, elevating the solution beyond trivial drone-sensing setups.

## 3. Project Features & Expected Deliverables

### 3.1. System Architecture & Technical Specifications
The system strictly fulfills the end-to-end IoT pipeline criteria:
* **Flight Platform & Actuation Layer:** Multi-rotor drone platform powered by an open-source Flight Controller (PX4 / ArduPilot). Autonomous waypoint navigation support and full flight telemetry integration via MAVLink into QGroundControl. Actuator control across ESCs/brushless motors.
* **Sensing & Embedded Processing Layer:** Dedicated ESP32 microcontroller interfaced via I2C with the BME280 sensor.
* **Downwash-Aware Sensing Mechanism (RQ1):** Comparative evaluation between an unshielded sensor and an aerodynamically ventilated pagoda shield across distinct flight regimes (Ground/OFF, Hover, and Forward Flight). Applies a lightweight Linear Regression calibration model to evaluate residual error reduction.
* **Adaptive Communication Mechanism (RQ2):** Dynamic sampling and transmission control FSM modulated by: RSSI signal strength, sensor-value variation rate ($\Delta$ Value), hysteresis thresholds, and Age of Information (AoI) constraints.
* **Connectivity & Visualization Layer:** Lightweight telemetry transmitted via MQTT protocol to a central database. Responsive web dashboard visualizing real-time environmental metrics, flight telemetry, and communication performance.

### 3.2. Core Research Questions & Provisional Targets
* **RQ1 (Downwash-Aware Sensing):** *How do UAV flight conditions and propeller downwash affect BME280 measurement accuracy, and can a ventilated pagoda shield effectively mitigate these aerodynamic measurement errors?*
  - **Target:** The shielded and calibrated sensor configuration aims to achieve a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) under $1.0^\circ\text{C}$ compared to a ground reference.
* **RQ2 (Adaptive Communication):** *Can an adaptive transmission policy based on RSSI, sensor variation, hysteresis, and AoI constraints significantly reduce aerial communication bandwidth while preserving data freshness (AoI) and packet delivery ratio (PDR)?*
  - **Target:** The adaptive policy provisional target is to achieve a **$30\% - 50\%$ packet reduction** compared to a fixed 1 Hz baseline, while sustaining an overall PDR of $\ge 95\%$.

### 3.3. Expected Deliverables
* **Integrated Hardware Artifact:** A functional, flight-tested IoT-UAV prototype integrating the flight controller, ESP32 telemetry unit, BME280 sensor, and custom 3D-printed ventilated pagoda shield.
* **Embedded Firmware & Web Software:** ESP32 embedded codebase implementing the adaptive pipeline, central database storage, and live Web Dashboard.
* **Empirical Experimental Report:** An IEEE-format technical report including quantitative sensing accuracy benchmarks and communication performance evaluation (PDR, Latency, AoI) across flight states.

### 3.4. Scope and Limitations (Known Constraints)
* **Flight Envelope Boundaries:** Flight experiments are strictly confined to low-altitude operations ($3 - 10\text{ m}$ above ground level) in an open test field during stable meteorological conditions for hardware safety.
* **Sensor Scope Isolation:** The research deliberately isolates ambient temperature and relative humidity via the BME280. Gas and chemical sensors (e.g., MQ series) are explicitly excluded to prevent cross-sensitivity artifacts and heater warm-up latencies from confounding the aerodynamic analysis.
